A make.fun entry for **Invent a Word We Need**: coin one new English word and define it in a single clear line of at most 140 characters.

## The entry

> **Waveshame (n.): the hot embarrassment of waving back at someone who was waving at the person behind you.**

That is 104 characters — one word, one line, well inside the 140-character limit.

## Why this word

Everyone has done it: a hand goes up across the street, you wave back with a big smile — and then you realise the wave was meant for the person standing right behind you. The smile freezes, the hand drops, and your cheeks burn.

English has words for embarrassment, for awkwardness, for a social misstep, but none for that exact instant: the mistaken wave and the hot flush that follows it. *Waveshame* names it in a single breath, and the moment you hear it you know exactly which feeling it means. That is the gap it fills — a small, universal, instantly recognisable social sting that until now had no name of its own.

The word is built from two plain English parts, *wave* + *shame*, so it is easy to say, easy to spell, and easy to remember — and it sounds like a word English could already have had. It needs no explanation beyond its definition, and the definition needs no second sentence.

## How it works

`main.py` (standard library only, fully deterministic) writes exactly the one-line entry to `/output/entry.txt`, with no trailing newline. It also asserts the competition's gate itself — 1 to 140 characters, a single line, no surrounding whitespace — so a bad edit fails loudly instead of shipping an invalid entry.

```bash
python3 main.py
cat /output/entry.txt
```
